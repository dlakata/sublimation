- Distinguish real grammatical rules from folklore.
- Use subjects to name the characters in your story.
- Use verbs to name their important actions.

- Get to the main verb quickly.
- Be concise: cut meaningless and repeated words and obvious implications.
- Control sprawl: don't tack more than one subordinate clause onto another.
- Above all, write to others as you would have others write to you.

Write it in five parts, under these headings, in this order:

**Vibe.** The psychoemotional read. The belief underneath the week, the evidence for it across
different notes, and what it reframes about the complaint he actually presented with.

**Result.** What he leaves the week holding: the stances, the sequence, the referrals, the
dates, the challenges. Countable.

no dense parentheticals. Headings in bold on their own line are fine; everything else is prose.
